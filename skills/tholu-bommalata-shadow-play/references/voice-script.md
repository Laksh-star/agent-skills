# Telugu Voice Script

How to write a recording script for a Telugu voice version of a shadow play. Write it as a document the user can edit, then ask for the recording first and retime the film to the voice (not the other way round).

## What a performance mixes

| Delivery | What it is | Register |
|---|---|---|
| గానం (sung) | Sanskrit ślokas for the invocation; short Telugu couplets in the manner of dwipada, the couplet form of the Ranganatha Ramayanam that troupes sing from | literary |
| వచనం (narration) | The sūtradhāra's linking prose between scenes | older literary register of mythological drama (చూచినాడు, పొండు, చేయుదును) |
| సంభాషణ (dialogue) | Each puppet's lines, spoken with energy | same literary register |
| హాస్యం (comic) | The clowns' interlude, addressed to the audience | everyday village speech |

## Script outline

1. **ప్రార్థన (invocation):** the Ganesha śloka (వక్రతుండ మహాకాయ…), the Saraswati śloka (సరస్వతి నమస్తుభ్యం…), then the sūtradhāra's greeting to the gathering and a one-line framing of the story.
2. **One section per scene:** a sung couplet where the emotion rises, narration to link, then dialogue. Label every line with its speaker.
3. **మంగళం (closing):** a short auspicious closing song and a one-line moral from the sūtradhāra.
4. **Optional comic interlude** between scenes, if the user wants one.
5. **Recording notes:** one file per scene named by number; a second of silence between speakers; spoken lines projected at a storyteller's pace, sung lines slower and held at line ends; anklet bells on hops and a foot-stamp at entrances if available; approximate length per section.

## Caveats to state in the script

- **Dialect.** Troupes perform in Telugu, but the puppeteer families speak Aare, a Marathi dialect, among themselves, and accounts describe traces of both in their singing. An agent can write the register, not that inflection; it must come from a performer.
- **Metre.** Couplets written "in the manner of" dwipada are not checked against the metre's rules (gana pattern, prasa, yati). A Telugu scholar or the performer should correct them.
- **Clown speech.** Regional speech written by an agent is an approximation; a performer from the region should rewrite it.
- **Opening and closing formulas** such as సభకు నమస్కారం and కథ కంచికి are common storytelling phrases, not documented conventions of this form.
- **Repertoire.** If the episode is outside the usual repertoire, there is no traditional wording to follow; say so.
- **Translation and dialogue are newly written**; the story's facts follow the source text.
